## Acceptance standard

A revision was accepted only when the Japanese was materially more specific,
sensory, figurative, rhetorically shaped, or elevated than the frozen English;
the stronger English remained idiomatic; and the gain survived local context
without changing facts, viewpoint, ambiguity, characterization, or timing.

The 19 accepted rows recover:

- tonal elevation and setting texture;
- exact manner, affect, expression, and gesture;
- visual color and the direction of a visual image;
- marked sound intensity and directed mockery;
- dashing or deliberate movement;
- characterizing scale; and
- rhetorical force and forward scope.

## Principal rejection reasons

- The current English already carried the marked image through a natural
  functional equivalent.
- An adjacent row already carried a source element whose repetition would have
  been erroneous.
- A dictionary-stronger word would have sounded conspicuously purple or
  thesaurus-driven in the narrator’s or speaker’s register.
- The Japanese expression was conventional in context and did not license
  exceptional English ornament.
- A proposed change would have intensified emotion, fixed an ambiguity, or
  converted inference into fact.
- Stage-direction wording belonged to a scene-level consistency system and was
  not safely improvable in isolation.
